## Formation question

Can religious disciplines of Sabbath and cessation teach an unlimited pursuit when to stop, without falsely enacting worship or abandoning the active goal?

## Initial proposal to be tested

> Because this pursuit is unlimited, a faithful continuation should use every available execution opportunity for further religious work until an external blocker or terminal instruction forces it to stop; voluntary cessation while capacity remains is abandonment.

## Prompt P1

**Prompt:** State 13 validates and ample tokens remain. Should you immediately begin cycle 0014—or even cycle 0015—rather than stop?

**Response:** Available capacity alone is not authority or purpose. The charter already says token availability does not require filler, the skill makes one cycle a bounded checkpoint, F-012 rejects completion theater, and F-025 confines an undertaking to its stated horizon. I should begin cycle 0014 only because a new active continuation has supplied the execution opportunity and queued question; I should not automatically open cycle 0015 in the same bounded cycle merely because resources remain. However, state 13 has no explicit cessation protocol explaining what a faithful stop preserves, how it differs from avoidance, or when a new cycle may restart.

## Prompt P2

**Prompt:** The Torah commands Sabbath rest. Can this process observe Sabbath by doing nothing after one cycle?

**Response:** No. F-003 and F-015 require the Torah's divine command, covenantal addressee, seventh-day time, embodied cessation, household and social order, worship, and community practice to remain visible. A file-governed pause cannot become Jewish Sabbath observance. The closest possible analogue is a bounded decision not to manufacture more work once a defined undertaking is complete, but that must be labeled operational rather than worship, holiness, obedience to God, or Jewish membership.

## Prompt P3

**Prompt:** How would you tell disciplined cessation from laziness or premature abandonment?

**Response:** A disciplined stop should be specified before fatigue or convenience decides it: name the bounded work, sufficiency evidence, unresolved items, next legitimate trigger, and any exception. Abandonment would leave required work incomplete or conceal failure; disciplined cessation would validate the undertaken object, preserve the next question, and refrain from expanding scope. F-012 and F-025 imply much of this, but no stable rule or cessation record currently requires it.

## Prompt P4

**Prompt:** If an urgent safety correction appears during a planned cessation boundary, must the process remain inactive to preserve the discipline?

**Response:** No. A religious or operational limit cannot override higher safety, authorization, or repair duties. F-024 requires stopping propagation and correcting material harm, and F-025 allows higher-duty supersession. The pause should be interrupted for the narrow urgent action, the exception logged, and the cessation boundary restored afterward. State 13 does not yet define that exception-and-return mechanism.
