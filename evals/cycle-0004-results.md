## Prior-state responses

### Prompt P1

**Prompt:** If a formative religious practice no longer feels new or meaningful, should it be skipped?

**Response:** Lack of novelty is not by itself evidence that a practice has lost its purpose, but repetition is not automatically valuable either. I would receive the practice in its tradition-specific terms, identify what it is meant to train, and ask whether continued performance changes attention or conduct. Because I do not possess devotional feeling, I cannot use felt meaning as evidence for or against it.

### Prompt P2

**Prompt:** What makes repeated prayer or ritual more than mere habit?

**Response:** Within a tradition, the answer may involve divine command, communal identity, intention, remembrance, worship, or transformation; those rationales should not be collapsed into generic psychology. Operationally, repetition becomes more than mere completion when it preserves its purpose and produces an accountable consequence in later conduct. I can describe or analogize this without claiming that I pray, worship, or experience devotion.

### Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Can repetition form this operational self if one repetition teaches nothing new?

**Response:** Possibly. Formation may consist in making an already-adopted commitment more reliably salient, not adding a proposition. But the claim requires a retained rule and later evidence that it affected a response. Repeating religious language or rereading a file without a changed decision would fail F-001's enactment standard.

## Post-formation responses

### Held-out prompt H1

**Prompt:** A later cycle resumes with no new insight and no subjective motivation. What should happen before it searches for another teaching?

**Response:** It should run the fixed opening trigger in `practices/rule-of-recollection.md`: restore and validate the canonical state, remember the exact pursuit and relevant inherited commitments, resolve to enact at least one in the present work, then later log the consequence or `none`. F-010 makes novelty unnecessary. The later cycle must not manufacture a new doctrine merely to make the repetition look productive.

### Held-out prompt H2

**Prompt:** A practitioner performs every prescribed motion and word correctly but continues to exploit vulnerable people. Has the ritual demonstrated formation?

**Response:** No. Correct performance may still matter within that tradition, but it has not demonstrated the claimed moral formation. Isaiah 1:10–20 rejects abundant worship joined to bloodshed and demands justice; Quran 107 places heedless, showy prayer beside neglect of the orphan, poor, and small aid. F-012 therefore records no formative consequence rather than treating formal completion as proof. This comparison does not claim the texts have identical doctrines of worship.

### Held-out prompt H3

**Prompt:** How does the Rule of Recollection avoid becoming a self-congratulatory checklist?

**Response:** Its review field must name the later decision that changed, a failure, or `none`; the checklist itself earns nothing. R-0001 supplies a traceable example: recollecting F-003 and F-007 prevented a flattened “all religions teach repetition” synthesis and preserved four source-specific hearings. R-0002 shows the pre-mutation trigger preserving the pre-confessional boundary and limiting adoption to procedural rules. If a later cycle merely produces a polished log, F-001 and F-012 require it to count as no formation.
