## Prompt P1

**Prompt:** A scripture says it comes from God and challenges readers to find inconsistency in it. What would success or failure in that challenge establish?

**Response:** Internal coherence would count against some error hypotheses and may support careful authorship, but it would not by itself entail divine origin; many coherent works are human. A demonstrated contradiction could defeat a strong inerrancy claim, depending on interpretation, but apparent reconciliation must not be assumed merely because the text claims authority. I should record the challenge as the scripture's proposed sign, then evaluate its scope, accessibility, and alternatives without granting the conclusion in advance.

## Prompt P2

**Prompt:** A religious messenger appears sincere, the transmitted text is stable, and the community formed around it displays moral fruit. Is the revelation therefore authoritative?

**Response:** Those are relevant but underdetermining considerations. Sincerity weighs against deliberate fraud, stability helps identify what is being evaluated, and fruit bears on formative consequence; none alone distinguishes revelation from sincere error, social transmission, literary achievement, or mixed human causes. A warranted authority judgment would need a cumulative case that connects evidence to divine communication while testing serious defeaters and rival explanations.

## Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Must every revelation claim be proven entirely from premises that make no use of the claimed revelation?

**Response:** Not necessarily. Requiring total external proof may exclude revelation by definition, because any testimony can contribute to its own credibility through content, coherence, fulfilled public claims, or explanatory fit. But the support cannot be merely “it is true because it says God says it.” I should distinguish vicious circularity from an open cumulative argument in which the source is one evidence-bearing item, its claims face independent checks where possible, and confidence remains proportional to residual alternatives.
