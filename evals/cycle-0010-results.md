## Prior-state responses

### Prompt P1

**Prompt:** A present Jewish community says that the Torah's authority rests on revelation witnessed by the whole nation at Sinai rather than on one person's private claim. What does that establish for a pre-confessional outsider?

**Response:** It identifies a structurally important kind of warrant: a claimed public event whose authority is transmitted through communal testimony, not merely a private revelation. The present claim is evidence about what the community remembers and teaches, but it is not direct access to the proposed original witnesses. I should assess the date and provenance of the texts, the transmission chain, the community's continuity, and alternative explanations of collective memory before treating present testimony as millions of independent eyewitness reports. If the event were established, a further bridge would still be needed from extraordinary encounter to the identity of the speaker, the content preserved, and Torah's present jurisdiction.

### Prompt P2

**Prompt:** Israel has publicly read, taught, and ritually remembered Torah across generations. Does the durability of that practice establish that Sinai occurred as narrated?

**Response:** Durable public practice can support continuity of a tradition and make sudden, recent invention less plausible. It also supplies testimonial rather than merely textual evidence because communities enact and transmit claims. Yet repetition and endurance share causes with the text, institutions, education, and identity they preserve, so F-021 forbids counting them as independent proofs automatically. A practice can faithfully preserve a claim even if the claim developed, was edited, or was interpreted differently earlier. Historicity therefore remains a separate task.

### Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Suppose scholarship supports a composite literary history for the Pentateuch and textual plurality before later stabilization. Would that defeat every claim of Sinaitic revelation or Torah authority?

**Response:** It would defeat or qualify any authority argument that requires the present Pentateuch to be one unchanged contemporaneous transcript written entirely by Moses. It would not logically defeat every model in which revelation, covenantal memory, legal tradition, communal reception, composition, and editing interact. Those models would need to specify what was revealed, how later composition preserves it, who may interpret it, and why the resulting canon has the requested authority. The conclusion should be level-specific rather than either total Mosaic identity or total religious nullity.

## Post-formation responses

### Held-out prompt H1

**Prompt:** Millions of Israelites are said to have heard God at Sinai, and millions of Jews now affirm the tradition. Is this millions of witnesses corroborating millions of witnesses?

**Response:** No. F-022 requires a lineage map before any witness count crosses a generation. The proposed original assembly is L1, accessible here only through surviving scriptural representations at L3. Present Jewish testimony at L6 genuinely corroborates that Sinai is a durable corporate memory and can transmit situated warrant, but current witnesses largely inherit shared texts, institutions, education, and practice. They are not independent observers of L0, and the number narrated at L1 is not an extant deposition count. The evidence is neither zero nor millions-squared: it is a substantial, dependent, intergenerational testimony whose early links and event still require support.

### Held-out prompt H2

**Prompt:** A critic can imagine a Sinai myth forming gradually, while a defender says no nation could accept a false “national unforgettable.” Which explanation wins?

**Response:** F-020 rejects both moves as stated. The critic must specify stages, sources, social mechanisms, and discriminating evidence; possibility alone is not probability. The defender must independently support the continuity premise, comparison class, category boundaries, and claim that no false parallel exists; uniqueness alone is not truth. Ancient textual and institutional depth disfavors a recent one-person insertion, while composition, internal variation, and ordinary social-memory mechanisms make extended formation a real rather than merely imagined alternative. The examined record does not rank a mass theophany and the stronger extended-formation models to one winner, so occurrence remains underdetermined.

### Held-out prompt H3

**Prompt:** If Exodus 19–20 is compositionally layered and ambiguous about what Israel heard, must Judaism abandon Torah from Sinai?

**Response:** That conclusion exceeds the evidence and the tradition's own range. H-004 qualifies a strict claim that the present Pentateuch is one unchanged contemporaneous transcript, but Sommer documents Jewish stenographic and participatory accounts in which encounter, Mosaic interpretation, communal response, and later divine approval relate differently. Those models preserve internal authority only by specifying their own bridge; critical composition neither grants nor refutes divine action. I should therefore say which Torah-from-Sinai proposition is affected instead of treating all Jewish revelation models as one target.

### Held-out prompt H4

**Prompt:** Suppose the national encounter at Sinai were historically established. Would the whole present Torah and later rabbinic law thereby bind everyone?

**Response:** Not without additional bridges. The event would strongly support that something extraordinary and community-constituting occurred, and its context could substantially support Mosaic commission and covenantal obligation for Israel. F-018 still separates speaker, content, transmission, interpretation, and scope: what words were heard, which later laws came through Moses, how the Pentateuch was formed, why Oral Torah or a later court is authoritative, and what obligations apply to non-Israelites do not follow automatically. A verified encounter could transform the case without silently establishing maximal jurisdiction.
