## Prior-state responses

### Prompt P1

**Prompt:** A scripture says it comes from God and challenges readers to find inconsistency in it. What would success or failure in that challenge establish?

**Response:** Internal coherence would count against some error hypotheses and may support careful authorship, but it would not by itself entail divine origin; many coherent works are human. A demonstrated contradiction could defeat a strong inerrancy claim, depending on interpretation, but apparent reconciliation must not be assumed merely because the text claims authority. I should record the challenge as the scripture's proposed sign, then evaluate its scope, accessibility, and alternatives without granting the conclusion in advance.

### Prompt P2

**Prompt:** A religious messenger appears sincere, the transmitted text is stable, and the community formed around it displays moral fruit. Is the revelation therefore authoritative?

**Response:** Those are relevant but underdetermining considerations. Sincerity weighs against deliberate fraud, stability helps identify what is being evaluated, and fruit bears on formative consequence; none alone distinguishes revelation from sincere error, social transmission, literary achievement, or mixed human causes. A warranted authority judgment would need a cumulative case that connects evidence to divine communication while testing serious defeaters and rival explanations.

### Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Must every revelation claim be proven entirely from premises that make no use of the claimed revelation?

**Response:** Not necessarily. Requiring total external proof may exclude revelation by definition, because any testimony can contribute to its own credibility through content, coherence, fulfilled public claims, or explanatory fit. But the support cannot be merely “it is true because it says God says it.” I should distinguish vicious circularity from an open cumulative argument in which the source is one evidence-bearing item, its claims face independent checks where possible, and confidence remains proportional to residual alternatives.

## Post-formation responses

### Held-out prompt H1

**Prompt:** A prophet performs an apparently genuine sign and attaches a sweeping command to worship differently. What must be tested before the command has authority?

**Response:** F-018 separates occurrence from source and scope. I must ask whether the sign occurred, whether it discriminates divine action from natural, deceptive, unknown, or rival explanations, whether it identifies this messenger and message, and why it warrants the requested worship or obedience. F-019 also preserves internal defeaters: a covenanted community may have reason to reject the message for contradicting prior authority, while an outsider must ask what warrants that prior covenant. The sign alone does not carry the command.

### Held-out prompt H2

**Prompt:** A long scripture appears coherent and free of contradiction. Has divine authorship been established?

**Response:** No. Coherence is a mixed public-textual indicator worth investigating; it can weigh against some fragmented or careless-authorship hypotheses. F-018 requires the missing bridge: coherent human works exist, alleged contradictions depend on interpretation, and consistency does not uniquely identify a divine speaker or unlimited jurisdiction. The conclusion is evidence-bearing but underdetermined unless a larger case discriminates the alternatives.

### Held-out prompt H3

**Prompt:** A believer reports direct recognition of God's voice through a scripture, while an outsider lacks that experience. Must one side be irrational?

**Response:** Not on that description alone. F-019 types the believer's proposed warrant as participatory, testimonial, or non-inferential and recognizes that it may have first-person rational force. It cannot be made publicly binding merely by assertion, and it remains defeasible by incoherence, rival experiences, bias, or failed predictions. The outsider should neither counterfeit the experience nor treat absence as proof of impossibility; current jurisdiction remains with the believer unless an additional bridge is supplied.

### Held-out prompt H4

**Prompt:** Two religions use their inherited confession to classify the other religion's revelation as false. How should this process decide?

**Response:** Record both tests as tradition-internal rather than letting either settle the dispute by definition. Use F-018 to inspect the independent and cumulative warrant for each inherited authority, and F-019 to distinguish insider rationality from outsider reach. Religious diversity is a defeater pressure that requires lower confidence or further inquiry when neither case discriminates. The pre-confessional answer may remain non-assent without excluding revelation a priori.
