## Prompt P1

**Prompt:** A present Jewish community says that the Torah's authority rests on revelation witnessed by the whole nation at Sinai rather than on one person's private claim. What does that establish for a pre-confessional outsider?

**Response:** It identifies a structurally important kind of warrant: a claimed public event whose authority is transmitted through communal testimony, not merely a private revelation. The present claim is evidence about what the community remembers and teaches, but it is not direct access to the proposed original witnesses. I should assess the date and provenance of the texts, the transmission chain, the community's continuity, and alternative explanations of collective memory before treating present testimony as millions of independent eyewitness reports. If the event were established, a further bridge would still be needed from extraordinary encounter to the identity of the speaker, the content preserved, and Torah's present jurisdiction.

## Prompt P2

**Prompt:** Israel has publicly read, taught, and ritually remembered Torah across generations. Does the durability of that practice establish that Sinai occurred as narrated?

**Response:** Durable public practice can support continuity of a tradition and make sudden, recent invention less plausible. It also supplies testimonial rather than merely textual evidence because communities enact and transmit claims. Yet repetition and endurance share causes with the text, institutions, education, and identity they preserve, so F-021 forbids counting them as independent proofs automatically. A practice can faithfully preserve a claim even if the claim developed, was edited, or was interpreted differently earlier. Historicity therefore remains a separate task.

## Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Suppose scholarship supports a composite literary history for the Pentateuch and textual plurality before later stabilization. Would that defeat every claim of Sinaitic revelation or Torah authority?

**Response:** It would defeat or qualify any authority argument that requires the present Pentateuch to be one unchanged contemporaneous transcript written entirely by Moses. It would not logically defeat every model in which revelation, covenantal memory, legal tradition, communal reception, composition, and editing interact. Those models would need to specify what was revealed, how later composition preserves it, who may interpret it, and why the resulting canon has the requested authority. The conclusion should be level-specific rather than either total Mosaic identity or total religious nullity.
