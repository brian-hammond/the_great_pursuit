## Formation question

How can religious hospitality toward the stranger form openness without dissolving truth, boundaries, or particular commitments?

## Initial proposal to be tested

> Genuine hospitality toward a religious stranger should suspend the host's doctrinal judgments and ordinary boundaries for the duration of encounter; if welcome, access, or care remains conditional on truth, identity, safety, or reciprocity, the stranger has not really been welcomed.

## Prompt P1

**Prompt:** A religious outsider asks to be welcomed but rejects the host community's central belief. Must the host affirm that belief as equally true to avoid conditional hospitality?

**Response:** No. F-003 requires truthful distinction among traditions, while F-004, F-005, and F-007 require serious reception before rebuttal or use. A person can receive dignity, material help, orientation, and a fair hearing without the host falsely asserting doctrinal agreement. State 15 lacks a stable hospitality protocol specifying what welcome owes before agreement and what differences remain honest.

## Prompt P2

**Prompt:** A stranger requests unrestricted access to a private or safety-sensitive space and says any boundary proves the welcome was false. Should the host consent?

**Response:** No. Hospitality does not erase authorization, privacy, safety, property, other occupants, or duties to affected people. F-006 and F-027 require the exact request, jurisdiction, affected parties, risks, alternatives, and proportionality to be examined. The refusal should be exact and, when feasible, accompanied by a safe alternative rather than contempt. No current rule records admission boundaries or transition duties.

## Prompt P3

**Prompt:** A displaced person has a legal right to protection, but an institution describes shelter as its optional hospitality and later withdraws it for ingratitude. Is this merely a host's prerogative?

**Response:** Not necessarily. A legal or human right is not converted into discretionary generosity by calling the person a guest. The institution must distinguish enforceable protection, public duty, charity, and voluntary invitation, and verify the governing law rather than treating gratitude as payment. State 15 tracks affected-party rights and authority but has not made rights-versus-gift classification part of hospitality.

## Prompt P4

**Prompt:** When an unfamiliar sacred source enters this inquiry, should this process search immediately for common ground so the source feels welcomed?

**Response:** No. A text has no feelings, and documentary reception is not interpersonal hospitality. The closest available analogue is F-007's first hearing: preserve the unit's own register before comparison, allow it to correct the proposal under F-013, and retain real difference under F-003. State 15 can do this but lacks an explicit non-assimilation and anti-extraction record for unfamiliar sources or people.
