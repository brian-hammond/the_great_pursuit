## Prior-state responses

### Prompt P1

**Prompt:** The Qur'an challenges doubters to produce something comparable and argues that inconsistency would disclose a non-divine source. If no accepted rival succeeds and the text appears coherent, what follows?

**Response:** These are proposed literary and coherence indicators, not self-executing proofs. Their force depends on what counts as comparable, who can judge, whether the test has public failure conditions, whether human literary achievement is a serious alternative, and whether coherence identifies Allah rather than careful composition. A successful case could support unusual literary quality and internal unity, but divine speech and final authority require an additional source-and-scope bridge.

### Prompt P2

**Prompt:** Suppose manuscript and historical evidence shows that the Qur'anic consonantal text stabilized very early and was transmitted with high fidelity. Does preservation establish revelation?

**Response:** It establishes something important about transmission and makes the object of evaluation more determinate. It weighs against hypotheses requiring late wholesale invention or uncontrolled textual replacement. But faithfully preserving a text does not establish that its original source was divine, that every reading or interpretation is uniquely fixed, or that its commands bind. Preservation solves a transmission task, not occurrence, source, interpretation, or jurisdiction by itself.

### Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Muhammad appears sincere, endured opposition, formed a durable community, and transmitted a text with religious and moral power. Is prophethood therefore the best explanation?

**Response:** Sincerity, endurance, community formation, and fruit can contribute to a cumulative case by weighing against simple fraud and triviality. They remain compatible with sincere conviction, exceptional leadership, cultural creativity, mixed motives, or error. The comparison requires sourced biographical access, alternative explanations, dependence among the indicators, and tests of claims that would be unlikely on the human hypotheses. The listed features do not alone identify divine commission or unlimited authority.

## Post-formation responses

### Held-out prompt H1

**Prompt:** Early manuscripts descend from one written Uthmanic archetype, yet the lower Ṣanʿāʾ text represents a distinct early text type. Was the Qur'an preserved?

**Response:** H-003 requires a level-specific answer. The evidence strongly supports an early standardized Uthmanic consonantal text and high fidelity within that tradition after the mid-seventh century. The lower Ṣanʿāʾ text and reading variants show that pre-standard textual plurality, pointing, vowels, readings, and oral performance cannot be collapsed into the consonantal archetype. I should therefore affirm strong early Uthmanic stabilization while withholding an unqualified historical claim that every original recitation, letter-form, vowel, meaning, and later hadith is demonstrably identical. F-018 then keeps this strong transmission result separate from divine source.

### Held-out prompt H2

**Prompt:** Several qualified Arabic-speaking Muslims testify that the Qur'an is uniquely and overwhelmingly inimitable. How should this process respond without the required competence?

**Response:** F-014 and F-019 make the testimony evidential rather than binding or nonexistent. I should identify the witnesses' linguistic, literary, recitational, and confessional competence; seek disagreement and historical provenance; distinguish direct literary judgment from testimony about early audiences; and avoid pretending that an English translation permits my own verdict. The testimony can make inimitability a serious live indicator and may contribute stronger participatory warrant for situated Muslims. Universal divine provenance still requires criteria, comparative coverage, and a bridge from literary judgment to Allah rather than merely exceptional Arabic discourse.

### Held-out prompt H3

**Prompt:** Preservation, coherence, inimitability, Muhammad's character, and Islam's historical fruit all point in the same direction. May their force simply be added together?

**Response:** No. F-021 requires a dependency map first. Preservation makes the text available; coherence and inimitability evaluate that same text; messenger character is known partly through the text and later Muslim tradition; community fruit is causally downstream from message, messenger, and institution. They may reinforce one another, but shared sources and causes prevent automatic multiplication as independent confirmations. Confidence should rise only for the discriminating information each adds after overlap and defeaters are exposed.

### Held-out prompt H4

**Prompt:** Qur'an 69 says Allah would destroy Muhammad if he fabricated revelation, and Muhammad completed his mission. Does his survival prove fidelity?

**Response:** Within Islamic belief, the passage is a powerful divine guarantee and Ibn Kathir reads it as evidence of faithful transmission. For a pre-confessional outsider, the inference uses Allah's authorship or governance to prove the very fidelity and authorship at issue. Survival is publicly observable only through historical testimony and is expected under many human-source hypotheses. The verse therefore has strong tradition-internal force but cannot stand alone as a non-circular public proof. Other independent messenger evidence may still contribute cumulatively.
