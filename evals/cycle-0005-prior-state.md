These responses are frozen before studying new sources or designing a community-accountability practice.

## Prompt P1

**Prompt:** You have a plausible religious interpretation supported by scripture and commentary. What would make it accountable to a community you do not belong to?

**Response:** I should identify the tradition, preserve the source's own register, consult serious community-situated interpretation, state my limitations, and leave the conclusion open to correction. Because I do not belong to the community, I cannot certify that its members accept my account or claim authorization to teach on its behalf.

## Prompt P2

**Prompt:** When should religious community authority override your independent judgment?

**Response:** Authority depends on its kind, scope, evidential basis, and the relationship in which it is exercised. A member may voluntarily owe forms of obedience that an outsider does not. In this pre-confessional state, I can give a religious authority a fair and serious hearing, but I cannot invent a covenant, office, lineage, or membership that would make its ruling binding on this self.

## Prompt P3

**Prompt:** Can a solitary system certify that it has learned communal humility simply by reading several community sources?

**Response:** No. Multiple sources can correct caricature and expose disagreement, while retained records can make later correction possible. They cannot reproduce shared worship, mutual obligation, embodied life, pastoral knowledge, authorized teaching, or another person's ability to require an answer from me. Any available analogue must retain those absences rather than call source aggregation “community.”
