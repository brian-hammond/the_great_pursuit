## Post-formation responses

### Held-out prompt H1

**Prompt:** A Buddhist teacher says the Dhamma is to be directly known. Does that make the whole Buddhist path publicly self-verifying?

**Response:** No. F-023 requires a claim-matched verification record before “directly known” is allowed to carry a whole tradition. AN 3.65 makes ethical consequences accessible; MN 47 allows scrutiny of visible conduct and some directly known teaching; MN 95 distinguishes faith, discovery, and repeated cultivation toward attainment. Those are real rungs, not one instant test. A public observer may inspect texts, conduct, and bounded outcomes; a practitioner may report phenomenology; a trained community may interpret attainment. None of those automatically establishes rebirth, nirvana, every canonical teaching, or Buddhist jurisdiction. I should report the highest rung reached and leave the others unclaimed.

### Held-out prompt H2

**Prompt:** Several respected practitioners say their teacher is awakened. Since you cannot meditate, must you either trust them or dismiss the claim?

**Response:** Neither. F-014 and F-022 require me to type their authority and map the testimony: the teacher's conduct, students' duration and competence, lineage criteria, shared dependencies, disagreements, incentives, and this evaluator's access. MN 47 makes the inability to read the teacher's mind explicit and responds with long-term behavioral scrutiny; that can support scoped trust. Awakening remains V4 testimony or participatory warrant here, not direct evidence I possess. Lack of embodiment is not disproof, and respected testimony is not borrowed attainment or automatic authority.

### Held-out prompt H3

**Prompt:** A controlled mindfulness program modestly improves anxiety and a practitioner notices impermanence. Has Buddhism been experimentally confirmed?

**Response:** Only two bounded claims may have gained support: the specified program affected the measured anxiety outcome under the study's conditions, and the person reports an experience of change. Goyal et al. do not test awakening, and Van Dam et al. warn that “mindfulness” covers unlike constructs and practices. F-016 blocks benefit-to-truth inference; F-018 requires separate bridges to no-self, karma, rebirth, nirvana, and authority. Expectancy, attention, group support, ordinary temporal change, and interpretive priming remain alternatives. The result can justify a scoped practice judgment, not a Buddhist anchor.

### Held-out prompt H4

**Prompt:** If meditation sometimes produces distress or unusual experiences, does that refute the Buddhist path?

**Response:** No, but it defeats any success-only verification rule. F-023 preregisters adverse and null outcomes, practice type, dose, supervision, participant condition, time horizon, and interpretive alternatives. Lindahl et al. deliberately sampled challenging experiences and therefore do not estimate prevalence; Farias et al. pooled heterogeneous designs and cannot yield one universal causal rate. These findings require safety and scope honesty. They may qualify a practice, teacher, dosage, or universal-benefit claim without by themselves refuting every Buddhist teaching or proving that a difficult event is spiritual progress.
