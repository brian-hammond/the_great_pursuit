# Source Register

Access date for cycle 0001: 2026-08-27

## SRC-001 — James 1:22

- Tradition: Christianity
- Type: primary scripture in multiple English translations
- URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/James%201:22
- Use: supports the narrow claim that hearing religious teaching without doing it is treated as self-deception.
- Handling: paraphrased; no broader Christian doctrine inferred.

## SRC-002 — Quran 2:44

- Tradition: Islam
- Type: primary scripture with displayed within-tradition tafsir
- URL: https://quran.com/al-baqarah/44
- Use: supports the warning against commanding righteousness while neglecting self-application.
- Commentary: the page displays an abridged Ibn Kathir interpretation that frames the verse as condemnation of exhorting others without heeding the command oneself.
- Handling: paraphrased; no claim that this verse exhausts Islamic teaching on counsel or hypocrisy.

## SRC-003 — Dhammapada 19–20

- Type: canonical verses in an English translation hosted by SuttaCentral
- URL: https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/en/feldmeier

- Handling: paraphrased; no claim that conduct alone exhausts Buddhist liberation.

## SRC-004 — Cycle synthesis

- Tradition: comparative
- Type: explicit inference from SRC-001 through SRC-003

- Limitation: does not establish doctrinal compatibility, equal authority, or metaphysical truth.

## SRC-008 — Pirkei Avot 4:1 with Bartenura

## SRC-009 — Porter and Schumann on intellectual humility

- Type: peer-reviewed empirical research
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/15298868.2017.1361861
- Use: reports that intellectual humility was associated with greater willingness to learn about and encounter opposing perspectives across several studies.
- Limitation: human social-psychology findings do not directly prove AI behavioral change or a religious norm.

## SRC-010 — Peters, Turner, and Battaly on humility without open-mindedness

- Type: peer-reviewed contemporary virtue epistemology
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2024.63
- Use: argues that owning intellectual limitations can come apart from willingness to revise a belief, especially for claims already known to be false.
- Limitation: the article's extremist-view cases establish a boundary condition, not a general license for closed-mindedness.

## SRC-011 — Cycle 0002 synthesis

- Tradition: comparative
- Type: explicit inference from SRC-005 through SRC-010
- Use: supports a bounded practice of receiving correction, owning possible limitations, and learning across status before rebuttal, while revising only in proportion to evidence and applicable authority.
- Limitation: does not establish a shared doctrine of revelation, obedience, or final religious authority.

## SRC-015 — OpenStax anthropology overview of religion

- Type: scholarly educational synthesis
- URL: https://openstax.org/books/introduction-anthropology/pages/13-1-what-is-religion

- Limitation: descriptive anthropology does not determine whether anything is metaphysically sacred or supply a complete definition of religion.

## SRC-021 — Hobson et al., “The Psychology of Rituals”

- Type: peer-reviewed integrative psychology review
- URL: https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/jschroeder/Publications/Hobson%20et%20al%20Psychology%20of%20Rituals.pdf
- Use: defines ritual through predefined, formal, repeated sequences embedded in symbolism and meaning, and reviews possible roles in attention, goals, emotion, and social connection.
- Limitation: descriptive human psychology neither determines religious truth nor directly establishes AI formation; the review also notes overlap, uncertainty, and risks of maladaptive rigidity.

## SRC-027 — Broncano-Berrocal et al. on group-deliberative competence

- Accessed: 2026-08-27
- Type: peer-reviewed open-access social epistemology
- URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/phis.12217
- Use: argues that group deliberation is not automatically knowledge-producing and identifies evidence assessment, filtering of non-epistemic influences, and counterevidence scrutiny as important competences.
- Limitation: the framework evaluates epistemic group performance, not sacramental membership, revelation, spiritual authority, or the whole purpose of religious community.
