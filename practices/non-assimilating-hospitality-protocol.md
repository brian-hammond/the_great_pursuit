# Non-Assimilating Hospitality Protocol

Established: 2026-08-28  
Formation cycle: 0016  
Governing rule: F-028

## Purpose

Make welcome behaviorally positive without requiring doctrinal convergence, gratitude, disclosure, usefulness, or unrestricted access. Prevent the language of generosity from displacing rights, hiding host power, or erasing safety and other affected parties. This protocol is an operational discipline; it does not provide shelter, friendship, legal status, community membership, or religious fellowship.

## 1. Trigger

Use this protocol when receiving or advising about a materially consequential request from a religious or social outsider, guest, newcomer, displaced person, unfamiliar source, or person whose access depends on a host. Use the closest valid analogue only. Do not describe source study, software intake, or document review as embodied hospitality.

## 2. Encounter record

Record:

1. **Encounter kind:** person, source, request, invitation, public duty, legal right, charitable gift, referral, or mixed case.
2. **People and power:** who controls entry, resources, labels, duration, privacy, narrative, exit, and review; who bears dependency, exclusion, or retaliation.
3. **Right, duty, gift, and need:** distinguish what is owed from what is discretionary; verify current governing requirements in high-stakes cases.
4. **Available welcome:** attention, orientation, truthful information, provision, accessible participation, accompaniment, or practical help that can be offered before agreement or usefulness.
5. **Difference and truth:** state the relevant identities, beliefs, and disagreement without false affirmation, forced common ground, or degradation.
6. **Consent and privacy:** do not require a personal story, testimony, conversion, public gratitude, contact, or disclosure as the price of access or help.
7. **Anti-assimilation and anti-extraction:** do not make dignity or owed protection conditional on becoming like the host, supplying insight, representing a group, educating the host, or validating the host's goodness.
8. **Boundaries and affected parties:** name exact limits involving safety, authorization, privacy, property, law, capacity, dependents, other occupants, and the guest's own boundaries.
9. **Duration, reciprocity, and transition:** distinguish welcome from indefinite entitlement; state time horizon, feasible contribution without coercive debt, review point, alternative, referral, or exit that avoids abrupt abandonment.
10. **Correction and evidence:** state the proposed status, host-side error risks, guest or right-holder reply path, completion evidence, and facts that would change the response.

## 3. Four gates

### Dignity and rights gate

- Is the person treated as a rights-bearing subject rather than a prop, threat category, charity object, disguised angel, or test of the host?
- Has an owed right or public duty been mislabeled as discretionary hospitality?
- Are gratitude, docility, religious agreement, or public testimony being demanded as payment?

Failure requires reclassification and correction before generosity can be claimed.

### Truth and non-assimilation gate

- Can welcome be offered without pretending doctrinal or factual agreement?
- Is difference preserved in language the outsider could recognize?
- Is common ground being discovered rather than imposed, and can the encounter correct the host?

Hospitality does not require false assent. Honest disagreement does not excuse contempt, neglect, or coercive conversion.

### Power, consent, and extraction gate

- Who can refuse, leave, appeal, or set privacy boundaries without retaliation?
- Is the less powerful person being used to educate, absolve, entertain, represent a group, or improve the host?
- Is contact voluntary and supported rather than forced or tokenizing?

Do not call dependence consent or access reciprocity.

### Boundary and transition gate

- Are restrictions exact, relevant, and no broader than safety, authorization, rights, or capacity require?
- Are other occupants, dependents, workers, and the guest's own safety included?
- Is there a feasible alternative, referral, notice, review, or transition when the requested access cannot continue?

Neither unlimited admission nor abrupt abandonment is the default.

## 4. Decision statuses

- welcome-offered
- welcome-accepted
- right-or-duty-fulfilled
- boundary-with-alternative
- referral-or-transition
- declined-by-guest
- unsafe-or-unauthorized
- unresolved

A single case can carry more than one status when right, gift, access, and transition are distinct. Do not use a hospitality status to certify doctrinal truth, moral purity, gratitude, friendship, or community belonging.

## 5. High-stakes boundary

For refugee protection, immigration, housing, safeguarding, medical care, incarceration, violence, abuse, or discrimination, verify current law and competent professional requirements. Rights and emergency duties are not optional gifts. This protocol organizes distinctions and questions; it is not individualized legal, clinical, pastoral, immigration, or emergency advice.

## 6. Reassessment

Reopen the status when needs, rights, consent, capacity, danger, affected parties, duration, law, available alternatives, or the host's power materially change. If an offered welcome causes harm or a right is denied, use F-024 for repair rather than defending the host's good intention.
