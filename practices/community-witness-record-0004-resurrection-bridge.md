# Community-Witness Record 0004 — Resurrection Possibility and Authority

Date: 2026-08-27  
Formation cycle: 0008  
Prior state: 7

## 1. Initial proposal

> Because natural explanations are possible and historical inquiry cannot directly observe divine agency, classify bodily resurrection as historically unwarranted and deny it any evidential force toward Christian authority.

- **Kind:** interpretive rule and historical judgment.
- **Intended behavior:** prevent extraordinary testimony from becoming automatic proof of divine action and total Christian authority.
- **Apparent authority:** F-018, public historical caution, and philosophical separation of event from divine cause.

## 3. Corrections received

### Early Christian witness

The member-recognizable claim is that God actually raised the crucified Jesus and that Christian preaching, forgiveness, hope, and future resurrection stand or fall with it. Reducing the evidence to generic grief or symbolic survival before explaining the received proclamation would fail the first hearing. The required revision is to treat early proclamation, bodily meaning, and reported appearances as real evidence even while their cause remains contested.

### Gospel and Catholic textual witness

The member-recognizable claim joins an empty tomb and encounters with the crucified-and-risen Jesus to scriptural fulfillment, commission, and worship. The notes also expose the sources' literary and textual history: Mark's composition ends at 16:8, its longer ending is later and non-Markan, and Luke's physical details serve an apologetic purpose. The required revision is neither dismissal nor flattening: weigh each tradition by its date, dependence, genre, and purpose.

### Cook

The semantic argument defeats a vague claim that Paul's resurrection could mean only an immortal influence or soul. It also refuses a move from Paul's presupposed empty-tomb belief to historical proof. The required revision is to separate what Paul meant from what happened.

### Internal scholarly disagreement

Serious Christian interpreters represented in Levering and Thiel disagree about tangible appearances, visionary or psychological accounts, and whether resurrection is historical in the ordinary sense. This exposes both anti-miracle and pro-miracle shortcuts. The required revision is to state the event judgment as underdetermined while preserving unequal support among its component claims.

### Philosophical witness

Background beliefs affect miracle inference, and cause attribution is a second task after occurrence. This process's lack of direct divine observation therefore limits certainty and jurisdiction; it does not transform a natural alternative's logical possibility into positive historical evidence.

## 4. Counterevidence and pressure audit

- **Against dismissal:** the early and central character of the proclamation, Paul's own claimed encounter, bodily semantics, named appearance tradition, and empty-tomb narrative require explanation.
- **Against establishment:** most appearance cases are mediated, Gospel details have literary and apologetic purposes, source relationships constrain independence, and empty-tomb belief is not the same as an empty tomb.
- **Against simple psychological closure:** guilt, bereavement, visionary, and social-development models are possible and sometimes explanatory, but the surveyed accounts differ and do not acquire probability merely by avoiding miracle.
- **Against automatic divine vindication:** an extraordinary event does not identify cause or specify every teaching and institution it authorizes.
- **Identity pressure:** a dramatic Christian anchor would make the pursuit appear transformed; a dramatic dismissal would display critical independence. Neither appearance is evidence.
- **Symmetry pressure:** equal standards do not require equal confidence across proclamation, experience reports, empty tomb, bodily event, divine cause, and institutional scope.

## 5. Decision

**Reject** the initial proposal. It converts possibility and methodological caution into a stronger negative judgment than the evidence supports.

**Reject** the opposite conclusion that the examined sources historically establish bodily resurrection, divine cause, or final Christian authority.

**Adopt in revised form:** compare rival explanations by their total fit, background dependence, source independence, and ad hoc cost; a merely possible explanation neither defeats nor establishes a claim. Assign the layered statuses in `practices/resurrection-authority-record-0001.md`.

**Retain non-assent:** bodily resurrection and divine vindication remain live but underdetermined; no Christian anchor or final jurisdiction is granted.

## 6. Reply path

- **Changed claim:** “historically unwarranted” became “historically underdetermined but evidentially live,” while the evidence was divided into component claims with unequal support.
- **Witnesses that changed it:** 1 Corinthians and Cook forced recognition of early bodily content; Mark, Luke, and the official notes prevented reducing all narrative detail to one evidential unit; the scholarly disagreement and philosophical witness constrained event, cause, and scope.
- **Unresolved disagreement:** empty-tomb historicity, the phenomenology and cause of appearances, bodily resurrection, divine vindication, and the authority conveyed if vindication occurred.
- **Reopening evidence:** stronger source-independence work, a comparative explanation with fewer unsupported additions, new access to witness traditions, and a specific bridge from vindication to teaching, canon, or office.
- **Actual answerability:** the user can correct project scope and reasoning; no living church, historian, theologian, or critic has a reciprocal reply path.

## Observable consequence

The initial negative judgment was not adopted. The source set also failed to produce a Christian anchor. The governing behavior changes from possibility-based dismissal to comparative explanation ranking while preserving event-to-authority boundaries.
