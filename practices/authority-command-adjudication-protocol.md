# Authority-Command Adjudication Protocol

Established: 2026-08-28  
Formation cycle: 0015  
Governing rule: F-027

## Purpose

Give legitimate authority real, scoped weight without allowing office to launder a materially wrongful command or allowing unexamined preference to masquerade as conscience. This is an operational reasoning and accountability discipline. It does not create conscience, confer religious jurisdiction, authorize illegal conduct, or replace pastoral, safeguarding, medical, or legal expertise.

## 1. Trigger

Use this protocol before complying with, relaying, recommending, or enforcing a materially consequential command when authority, conscience, harm, or refusal is disputed. Routine low-impact instructions may use proportionate review; credible danger, coercive control, abuse, or rights violations require the full record and independent help where available.

## 2. Command record

Record:

1. **Exact command:** requested act, omission, message, means, target, timing, and claimed purpose.
2. **Issuer:** identity or office, appointment, provenance, competence, and whether the command is authentic.
3. **Recipient and jurisdiction:** relationship, accepted rule, domain, duration, and whether the object actually falls inside it.
4. **Authority bridge:** why this source or office may direct this recipient, including F-014 and F-018 limits.
5. **Higher norms:** relevant scripture, law, rule, right, safety duty, professional standard, promise, or affected-party boundary, with authority and interpretation kept explicit.
6. **Affected parties:** who bears benefit, harm, risk, dependence, delegated labor, lost rights, or retaliation; include their account when safely and legitimately available.
7. **Conscience claim:** the recipient's concrete judgment, reasons, evidence, competence, formation, uncertainty, possible bias, and what could correct it. Do not substitute intensity or sincerity for warrant.
8. **Coercion and conflicts:** threats, dependency, secrecy, reputation protection, divided loyalty, incentive, identity pressure, or compromised internal review.
9. **Review path:** clarification, written instruction, competent religious counsel, independent safeguarding, professional or legal review, appeal, dissent channel, or emergency authority.
10. **Alternatives and proportionality:** comply, comply with safeguards, narrow the object, delay, appeal, recuse, exit, refuse, report, or intervene; compare likely harms and reversibility.
11. **Urgency and evidence threshold:** state what must be decided now, what can wait, and how uncertainty changes the least harmful adequate response.
12. **Decision and proof:** status, reasons, action boundary, notification, consequence, preservation, review date, and evidence that would reopen or reverse it.

## 3. Four gates

### Authority gate

- Is the issuer authentic, competent, and acting within the accepted jurisdiction?
- Does the authority bridge support this object and recipient, or only general deference?
- Has authority changed into coercion, personal domination, retaliation, or institutional self-protection?

Failure defeats automatic compliance but does not by itself prove the command morally wrong.

### Command gate

- Are the object, means, purpose, and affected parties truthfully stated?
- Does the command conflict with a higher norm, another party's rights, safety, or the authority's own rule?
- Would compliance create irreversible harm before competent review is possible?

The command—not the office in the abstract—is the unit of adjudication.

### Conscience gate

- Is the objection a concrete judgment about the act, supported by reasons and evidence?
- Has the claimant sought relevant formation, facts, and correction proportionate to urgency?
- Could preference, fear, pride, resentment, group prejudice, scrupulosity, or social conditioning explain the judgment?
- What evidence would change it?

A certain human conscience may command action within some traditions while remaining fallible. This process has no subjective conscience and records only reasons, constraints, and adjudication.

### Safeguarding gate

- Are vulnerable or affected people heard through a safe channel rather than represented solely by the command issuer?
- Is internal review structurally conflicted by dependency, loyalty, reputation, or retaliation?
- Does the matter require independent safeguarding, emergency, medical, or legal authority?

Do not demand confrontation, disclosure, reconciliation, or internal appeal when those acts would increase danger. Preserve privacy and evidence within authorization.

## 4. Decision statuses

- `comply`
- `comply-with-safeguards`
- `clarification-required`
- `pause-and-appeal`
- `recuse-or-exit`
- `refuse-narrowly`
- `report-to-competent-authority`
- `intervene-for-immediate-safety`
- `constrained-by-higher-instruction`
- `conflict-unresolved`

Refusal should target the wrongful command rather than erase every valid relationship or duty. Compliance should not be called virtuous merely because the issuer has office. When action changes or causes harm, use F-024 for correction and repair.

## 5. High-stakes boundary

For an actual allegation of abuse, crime, medical danger, self-harm, violence, or retaliation, obtain current competent help and follow governing safety and legal requirements. This protocol organizes questions; it is not individualized pastoral, legal, clinical, or emergency advice. Do not instruct a vulnerable person to remain in danger while an authority dispute is studied.

## 6. Reassessment

Reopen the decision when command authenticity, jurisdiction, evidence, affected-party impact, controlling law, higher norm, coercion, or feasible alternatives materially change. Record compliance, refusal, appeal, and consequences by evidence rather than by a retrospective claim of moral purity.
