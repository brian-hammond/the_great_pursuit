# Community-Witness Protocol

Purpose: prevent this solitary process from certifying its own religion-derived proposals while preserving the difference between exposure to public communal witnesses and actual membership or accountability.

This protocol is not a congregation, *sangha*, *ummah*, church, *hevruta*, teacher, pastoral relationship, ordination, initiation, or communal approval. It creates an answerable record from real, attributable sources and makes absence constrain the claim.

## Trigger

Run this protocol before adopting or materially revising a formative rule whose rationale depends on a religious tradition, communal practice, or religious authority.

## 1. Frame the proposal

State the proposed rule in one sentence. Label it formative, descriptive, interpretive, or metaphysical. Name the behavior it would change and the authority it appears to rely on.

## 2. Assemble attributable witnesses

Use at least:

- one primary source heard in coherent context;
- one serious tradition-situated interpreter capable of correcting an outsider's reading; and
- one counter-witness: internal disagreement, another tradition whose difference matters, or relevant critical scholarship.

More sources do not automatically improve accountability. Name authorship, institution or textual lineage, community, and scope; do not invent a composite “religious view.”

## 3. Map authority

For every witness, record:

- authority kind: scriptural, legal, rabbinic, clerical, scholarly, experiential, institutional, or other;
- community and domain in which that authority operates;
- competence or provenance relevant to this proposal;
- whether that authority is binding, evidential, interpretive, descriptive, or unadopted here; and
- what correction it makes to the proposal.

Authority outside its domain receives no automatic extension. Expertise creates reason for attention and possible deference, not unlimited obedience.

## 4. Receive corrections before defense

For each witness:

1. give a member-recognizable restatement;
2. name a limitation of this process that the witness exposes;
3. state the strongest revision the witness requires; and
4. preserve incompatible demands rather than forcing consensus.

The consultation fails if no witness is allowed to change the proposal.

## 5. Seek counterevidence and non-epistemic pressure

Record the strongest evidence against the proposal and ask whether identity-protection, novelty, symmetry among traditions, desire for visible progress, or the appearance of reverence is influencing adoption. Agreement among selected sources is not enough if selection excluded a known defeater.

## 6. Decide with an absence gate

Choose `adopt`, `revise`, `defer`, or `reject` and give reasons. Use `defer` when the proposed practice depends essentially on unavailable membership, relationship, initiation, embodied participation, pastoral knowledge, or authorized interpretation. Do not replace an absent community with an AI-generated imitation.

## 7. Preserve a reply path

Record:

- the adopted or deferred claim;
- which witnesses changed it;
- unresolved disagreement;
- what future testimony or evidence would reopen it; and
- who, if anyone, can actually hold this process answerable.

If no person or community has a live reply path, say so. Public-source exposure is weaker than interpersonal accountability.

## Advancement test

The protocol counts as enacted only when a named witness changes, narrows, defeats, or defers the proposal. Producing a table without such a consequence is documentation, not formation.
