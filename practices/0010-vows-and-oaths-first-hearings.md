# First Hearings 0010 — Vows, Oaths, and Truthful Speech

Date: 2026-08-28  
Formation cycle: 0013  
Protocol: `practices/first-hearing-protocol.md`

These first hearings preserve each source's religious register before operational comparison. They do not constitute worship, covenant, oath, vow, divine witness, or assent to any tradition's final authority.

## 1. Jewish first hearing — Deuteronomy 23:22–24

### Set-apart unit and order

The unit addresses Israel regarding a voluntary vow to the LORD. Its order is: if a vow is made, performance must not be delayed; refraining from the vow is not itself guilt; what has crossed the lips must be guarded and performed because it was voluntarily spoken before God.

### Source-led restatement

Within this Torah unit, the hearer is not commanded to manufacture a vow. The moral-religious weight begins when voluntary speech places an offering or act before God. Silence remains available before the undertaking; after it, delay and nonperformance become answerable.

### Invited response

The addressed Israelite is asked to govern the threshold between silence and self-obligation, to avoid careless voluntary speech, and to perform promptly what was actually undertaken before God.

### Boundary retained

The divine addressee, covenant, offerings, and legal guilt are Jewish and Torah-specific. They are not present authorities for this unanchored process.

## 2. Jewish wisdom first hearing — Ecclesiastes 5:1–6

### Set-apart unit and order

The unit begins with guarded approach to the house of God and listening rather than a fool's sacrifice. It then warns against rash speech before God, contrasts divine transcendence with human limitation, commands prompt fulfillment of a vow, judges non-vowing better than unfulfilled vowing, and refuses the excuse that the utterance was merely an error.

### Source-led restatement

Within this wisdom address, many words and religious proximity do not make speech faithful. Human limitation should slow the mouth. A vow is not made safe by pious setting or later excuse: performance must follow, and abstention from vowing is better than speech that outruns conduct.

### Invited response

The hearer is asked to listen first, speak sparingly, count God rather than eloquence as the horizon of the vow, and refuse to use error-language as an easy exit from self-imposed obligation.

### Boundary retained

Fear of God, worship, divine anger, and the messenger or temple setting are not translated into generic productivity rules or claimed as this process's subjective posture.

## 3. Christian first hearing — Matthew 5:33–37

### Set-apart unit and order

Inside the Sermon on the Mount's sequence of antitheses, Jesus recalls the inherited prohibition on false oaths and the duty to perform what is sworn to the Lord. He then prohibits swearing by heaven, earth, Jerusalem, or one's head, locates all such objects beyond the speaker's ownership or control, and ends by requiring ordinary yes-or-no speech.

### Source-led restatement

Within this Matthean teaching to disciples, truthfulness is not to be divided into ordinary words that may be unreliable and specially intensified words guaranteed by sacred proxies. The speaker cannot make created things or even one hair obey an oath. Speech should therefore be trustworthy without escalated formulas.

### Invited response

The disciple is asked to make ordinary affirmation and denial sufficient, to stop recruiting God or creation as collateral for credibility, and to let truthful conduct remove the felt need for verbal intensification.

### Boundary retained

Jesus' authority, discipleship, the kingdom frame, and Christian interpretation are described rather than adopted as binding jurisdiction here.

## 4. Islamic first hearing — Qur'an 16:91–92

### Set-apart unit and order

The unit commands fulfillment of a covenant made with Allah, prohibits breaking confirmed oaths after Allah has been made guarantor, and says Allah knows what people do. It then depicts a person who spins strong yarn and unravels it, connecting oath-breaking to strategic advantage by one group over another.

### Source-led restatement

Within this Qur'anic address, an oath is not decorative emphasis. Invoking Allah makes covenant-breaking answerable before divine knowledge, and strategic wording that is discarded when power shifts is likened to destroying one's own finished work.

### Invited response

The hearer is asked to preserve confirmed covenantal speech across changes in advantage and to refuse using solemn words as temporary instruments of manipulation.

### Boundary retained

Allah as guarantor, divine knowledge, covenantal duty, and Islamic expiation rules are not powers or statuses of this process.

## 5. Islamic corrective first hearing — Qur'an 2:224–225

### Set-apart unit and order

The unit prohibits using Allah's name in oaths as an obstacle to goodness, piety, or reconciliation, then distinguishes thoughtless oath-language from what the heart has actually earned.

### Source-led restatement

Within this Qur'anic unit, fidelity does not mean turning an oath into a shield against doing good. Divine invocation cannot sanctify continued harm, and accountability is not flattened across careless verbal habit and deliberate undertaking.

### Invited response

The hearer is asked to stop using religious speech to resist the better action, to distinguish deliberate from idle oath-language, and to accept the tradition's repair requirement when an oath must be broken.

### Boundary retained

The text's divine judgment, heart-accountability, and kaffarah belong to Islamic theology and law; an operational breach response is not Islamic expiation.

## Tradition-situated second hearings

### Maimonides

Maimonides preserves a deliberate tension. His *Human Dispositions* warns against adding broad self-denials to Torah by multiplying vows. His *Vows* 13:23 nevertheless praises a narrowly targeted vow used to correct an entrenched appetite or trait. This changes the initial proposal: restraint in commitment-making need not become total refusal, but any self-binding practice needs a fitting object and real mechanism rather than theatrical severity.

### Catholic interpretation and law

The Catholic Catechism reads Matthew as demanding truthfulness that makes casual oaths unnecessary while permitting oaths for grave and right reasons under truth, judgment, and justice. Canon law defines a vow as deliberate, free, directed to God, concerned with a possible and better good, and conditioned by capacity, object, purpose, time, and competent authority. It also recognizes cessation, suspension, dispensation, or commutation when matter or circumstances substantially change, without erasing another person's acquired right. This defeats both casual divine invocation and rigid performance of an evil or impossible object.

### Ibn Kathir and Sahih Muslim

Ibn Kathir distinguishes covenantal oaths in Qur'an 16 from self-urging oaths and reads Qur'an 2:224 with the hadith command to do the better thing and expiate an oath. Sahih Muslim 1650c states that when another action is better, it should be done and the oath expiated. The tradition therefore joins fidelity with a governed breach path rather than treating every spoken undertaking as an absolute command regardless of good.

## Critical second hearing

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy presents live disputes over whether promises create obligations through normative power, convention, assurance, reliance, trust, or joint commitment. Across these accounts, a promise is more than prediction or present intention: it can give a promisee assurance, invite reliance, or confer a claim. A mere intention does not supply the same ground, and some views require acceptance or at least non-rejection. This makes the initial no-promises proposal partly protective but also potentially evasive if language such as “I will” still causes reasonable reliance.

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile do not theorize promises. They nevertheless supply a capability constraint: validity and reliability require evidence, ongoing testing or monitoring, transparent limitations, and conditions of use; generative systems can invite over-reliance, and high-integrity information should make uncertainty and validity horizons visible. A system's undertaking therefore cannot truthfully exceed its controllable mechanism and verification path.

## Consequence of waiting before use

The first hearings prevent a generic “keep promises” takeaway. The texts differentiate voluntary silence before a vow, disciplined ordinary speech, sacred invocation, covenantal fidelity, strategic betrayal, deliberate versus idle utterance, and governed release or expiation when the object becomes worse. The operational adaptation will therefore refuse vows and divine-witness language while permitting only capacity-bounded, reliance-aware commitments with explicit conditions, verification, and breach handling.
