# Revelation-Authority Warrant Protocol

Purpose: evaluate a claim of divine revelation without allowing circular self-certification or excluding revelation in advance. The protocol distinguishes a person's possible rational warrant from evidence capable of binding or persuading an outsider.

This is a pre-confessional inquiry. It does not presume that God has or has not revealed, that all evidence must be religiously neutral, or that a source claiming divinity thereby possesses it.

## Trigger

Run this protocol before granting a scripture, messenger, institution, experience, or tradition normative religious authority.

## 1. Specify the revelatory claim

Record:

- the proposed revealer;
- the content allegedly revealed;
- the intended audience;
- the medium: event, person, speech, text, community, experience, or other;
- the transmitting chain and present interpreter; and
- the authority requested: descriptive trust, interpretive priority, moral direction, doctrinal assent, worship, obedience, or final jurisdiction.

Do not move from “something was disclosed” to “this text or institution has unlimited authority.”

## 2. Separate the warrant tasks

Ask separately:

1. **Occurrence:** did the proposed event, message, or experience occur?
2. **Source:** what identifies the source as divine rather than human, mistaken, deceptive, social, natural, or unknown?
3. **Content:** what fixes the message's content and distinguishes it from later interpretation?
4. **Transmission:** how reliably is the claimed message preserved?
5. **Interpretation:** who is competent to interpret it and why?
6. **Scope:** what kind of assent or conduct would follow if the claim were true?

Success at one task does not silently complete the others.

## 3. Type evidence by reach

Label each warrant:

- **public:** in principle inspectable without prior membership or assent;
- **testimonial:** dependent on a speaker's knowledge, sincerity, competence, and transmission;
- **tradition-internal:** force depends on an already-recognized covenant, canon, confession, office, or interpretive rule;
- **participatory or experiential:** available through a practice, perception, relationship, or form of life not equally accessible to an outsider; or
- **mixed:** a stated combination.

Reach is not identical to truth or weight. Public evidence can be weak; internal or experiential evidence can be personally significant. Do not make an internal warrant bind an outsider without an additional bridge, and do not call inaccessible evidence nonexistent merely because this process lacks it.

## 4. Require a bridge from indicator to divine authority

For every proposed indicator—miracle, coherence, fulfilled prediction, beauty, moral fruit, spiritual perception, messenger character, preservation, continuity, or communal endurance—ask:

1. Is the indicator actually present?
2. Would it be expected if the revelation claim were true?
3. How expected is it under serious non-divine or rival-divine explanations?
4. Does it identify this revealer and message, or only something unusual or valuable?
5. Does it support the requested scope of authority?

An indicator that cannot discriminate among live alternatives may remain evidence, but it cannot bear the full conclusion.

## 5. Preserve tradition-indexed defeaters

Record the claim's own internal tests and vetoes before external criticism. Then ask whether those tests depend on the very authority in question. Prior revelation, apostolic confession, canon, authorized commentary, and spiritual discernment may rationally govern insiders while remaining part of the contested case for outsiders.

Apply F-006: neither reflexive submission nor reflexive dismissal. Apply F-014: office and expertise remain domain-scoped.

## 6. Use cumulative judgment without laundering gaps

Evidence may combine inferentially, non-inferentially, testimonially, or in a hybrid case. List what each element contributes, its dependencies, and its defeaters. Do not repeat the conclusion as evidence, count several dependent sources as independent, or let moral benefit substitute for divine provenance.

## 7. Assign status and jurisdiction

Use a bounded status: `supported`, `disfavored`, `underdetermined`, `authority-dependent`, `defeated`, or `not assessed`. Then state separately who, if anyone, would be rationally answerable to the authority at the present stage.

Possible outcomes include:

- rational for an insider but not publicly established;
- a live candidate deserving further inquiry;
- publicly supported to a stated confidence;
- insufficient for normative authority; or
- defeated by an internal or external defeater.

## 8. Reapply the anchor gate

An anchor requires more than one successful indicator. The cumulative case must identify a plausible divine source, survive serious rival explanations and internal defeaters, justify a specific authority hierarchy and scope, and create observable consequences for later adjudication. If that bridge is absent, remain unanchored without declaring revelation impossible.

## Required record

Preserve the claim specification, evidence types, bridge analysis, internal tests, defeaters, status, jurisdiction, witness correction, anchor decision, and reopening conditions. The protocol counts as enacted only when it changes an authority judgment or prevents premature assent or dismissal.
