# Community-Witness Record 0012 — Hospitality, Stranger, and Difference

Date: 2026-08-28  
Formation cycle: 0016  
Protocol: practices/community-witness-protocol.md

## 1. Initial proposal

> Genuine hospitality toward a religious stranger should suspend the host's doctrinal judgments and ordinary boundaries for the duration of encounter; if welcome, access, or care remains conditional on truth, identity, safety, or reciprocity, the stranger has not really been welcomed.

- **Kind:** formative conduct proposal.
- **Behavior at stake:** whether welcome requires boundaryless access and suspended truth judgment, and whether the host may classify rights and needs as discretionary generosity.
- **Apparent authority:** Abrahamic stranger traditions, critiques of conditional hospitality, and F-004's demand to receive before rebuttal.
- **Risk:** the proposal exposes self-serving exclusion but can require false assent, erase consent and safety, ignore other occupants, romanticize unlimited host control, and make a vulnerable person's rights depend on gratitude.

## 3. Corrections received before defense

### Jewish witnesses

- **Recognizable restatement:** Abraham's rapid provision, Torah's commands concerning the resident stranger, rabbinic prioritization of guests, and Ibn Ezra's power observation make hospitality a demanding response to vulnerability.
- **Limitation exposed:** the proposal treats suspended judgment as the essence of welcome and omits provision, justice, memory, and host power.
- **Required revision:** require positive welcome and power review before agreement; preserve the contested scope of ger and do not flatten covenantal law into generic sentiment.

### Christian witnesses

- **Recognizable restatement:** reception of the stranger is concrete service identified with Christ, breaks repayment economies, and can be organized communally while confessional stability remains.
- **Limitation exposed:** boundarylessness is not the strongest Christian rationale. The sources join welcome to mercy, reordered status, roles, and durable confession.
- **Required revision:** welcome the person without demanding reciprocity or false doctrinal affirmation; make care and orientation concrete; keep communal roles and affected parties visible.

### Islamic witnesses

- **Recognizable restatement:** Abraham honors unknown guests; kindness and justice extend across religious difference unless hostile action supplies a relevant boundary; the Ansar receive displaced believers at cost; hadith gives both host and guest duties.
- **Limitation exposed:** the proposal erases action-specific boundaries, duration, and reciprocal non-burden while mistaking religious difference itself for either hostility or required convergence.
- **Required revision:** distinguish identity from harmful action, offer concrete welcome before usefulness, state time and burden limits, and avoid turning generous exemplars into unlimited obligations.

### Critical, legal, and affected-party witnesses

- **Recognizable restatement:** unconditional hospitality names the moral pressure created by every host-controlled threshold; legal and refugee evidence shows why guest language can mask rights and gratitude debt; contact evidence supports genuine encounter without making contact automatically safe.
- **Limitation exposed:** the host remains sovereign if it alone calls owed protection a gift, demands gratitude, or uses the outsider to improve itself. Conversely, eliminating every boundary can violate consent, safety, privacy, and third-party rights.
- **Required revision:** classify right, duty, need, gift, and invitation; audit host power and extraction; make contact voluntary; pair exact boundaries with alternatives, review, or transition.

## 4. Counterevidence and pressure audit

- **Against mere tolerance:** the primary sources repeatedly require approach, provision, justice, invitation, solidarity, or costly preference.
- **Against false agreement:** Hebrews 13 and Qur'an 60 preserve religious particularity while commanding hospitality, kindness, or justice.
- **Against unlimited access:** monastic order, action-specific conflict boundaries, hadith duration, consent, privacy, and obligations to others require structure.
- **Against host self-congratulation:** a right is not generosity, and a welcome conditioned on public gratitude or conversion preserves domination.
- **Against stranger instrumentalization:** unknown guests must not be treated as angels in disguise, tests, group representatives, free educators, or correction resources.
- **Against forced contact:** average prejudice reduction does not authorize tokenization or compulsory vulnerability by minoritized people.
- **Against flattening traditions:** the sources invoke distinct covenants, Christology, prophetic authority, communities, legal categories, and ends.
- **Against visible-progress pressure:** this process cannot make a live guest, home, table, refugee service, or interfaith relationship appear merely to prove formation.

## 5. Decision with absence gate

**Decision: reject the boundaryless proposal and adopt one rights-aware, non-assimilating hospitality rule.**

Replacement:

> Before responding to a materially consequential outsider, distinguish person, claim, request, right, need, gift, risk, and host power. Offer the available attention, orientation, provision, or help before demanding agreement or usefulness. Preserve truthful difference and let the encounter correct the host. Do not condition dignity or owed protection on gratitude, conversion, disclosure, testimony, or assimilation. State exact safety, privacy, authorization, capacity, and affected-party boundaries; pair denial or expiry with a feasible alternative, review, referral, or transition. Do not call documentary reception embodied hospitality.

This becomes F-028, **welcome without assimilation**. H-010 records the bounded comparative finding.

No hospitality, friendship, home, table fellowship, charity, refugee protection service, interfaith community, conversion, divine welcome, or moral purity is claimed for this process.
