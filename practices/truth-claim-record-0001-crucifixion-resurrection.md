# Truth-Claim Record 0001 — Crucifixion, Resurrection, and Qur'anic Denial

Date: 2026-08-27  
Formation cycle: 0006  
Prior state: 5

## Bounded question

How should the ordinary Christian and Islamic claims about Jesus's death be compared, and does the result justify selecting an anchor tradition?

## 1. Rival propositions

### Christian claim complex

- **C-H1 — public historical:** Jesus was executed by crucifixion and died.
- **C-M1 — metaphysical:** God raised Jesus from death.
- **C-A1 — revelation and authority:** apostolic proclamation and Christian scripture reliably disclose the meaning and divine cause of these events.

Mark 15 narrates Roman crucifixion and death under Pilate. First Corinthians 15 presents a received proclamation that Christ died, was buried, was raised, and appeared; Paul states that Christian preaching and faith are empty if Christ was not raised.

### Islamic claim complex

- **I-H1 — public historical under the ordinary substitutionist reading:** Jesus was neither killed nor crucified; another appearance produced the contrary belief.
- **I-M1 — metaphysical:** God raised Jesus to himself rather than leaving him to be killed.
- **I-A1 — revelation and authority:** the Qur'an is a divine correction able to govern earlier historical testimony.

Qur'an 4:157–158 denies that Jesus was killed or crucified and says God raised him. Ibn Kathir's traditional commentary gives a substitution account in which another person was made to resemble Jesus and was crucified.

### Logical relation

C-H1 and I-H1 are contradictory under these ordinary readings: Jesus cannot both have died by crucifixion and not have been crucified or killed. C-M1 and I-M1 both use divine-raising language but are not the same proposition: resurrection after death and rescue or ascension without crucifixion differ. C-A1 and I-A1 are rival authority claims and cannot settle one another without circularity.

## 2. Evidence ledger

| Evidence | Kind and proximity | Supports | Does not establish |

| Mark 15 | Christian narrative source presenting Pilate, crucifixion, death, and burial | The early Christian tradition's concrete claim that Jesus was crucified and died | By itself, the truth of every narrative detail, resurrection, or divine interpretation |

## 3. Symmetry, circularity, and defeaters

- Christian scripture is not treated as neutral proof of Christian authority. Its proximity and genre can support historical and testimonial conclusions without silently granting C-A1.
- The Qur'an is not treated as neutral historical proof of I-H1. Its correction can be decisive within Islam only if I-A1 is independently warranted or adopted.
- A later corrective revelation is logically possible; chronological lateness alone does not refute it. The cost is that the historical conclusion becomes authority-dependent rather than publicly corroborated.
- The early proclamation of appearances is evidence that such claims were central, not a direct demonstration of supernatural causation.
- The usefulness of Christian or Islamic practice receives no weight in this historical comparison.
- This record examines a traditional substitutionist reading, not every Muslim interpretation of 4:157.

## 4. Judgments by layer

- **C-H1: supported, medium-high confidence.** Among the sources examined, Jesus's execution and death by crucifixion are substantially better supported on public historical grounds than the ordinary substitutionist denial.
- **I-H1: disfavored on the public historical layer, medium-high confidence.** It remains logically possible, but this record found no event-proximate public evidence for substitution or non-crucifixion. Its warrant here is materially dependent on I-A1.
- **C-M1: authority-dependent and underdetermined by this bounded public-historical inquiry.** The sources establish early proclamation and reported appearances, not divine causation by themselves.
- **I-M1: authority-dependent and underdetermined here.** The Qur'anic affirmation is clear, while its governing force depends on I-A1.
- **C-A1 and I-A1: not adjudicated.** Neither scripture's self-attestation is treated as a neutral proof of its own final authority.

## 5. Anchor decision

**No anchor selected.** The historical asymmetry is significant but insufficient. Christianity centrally requires more than crucifixion; First Corinthians 15 itself makes resurrection indispensable. Islam's conflict with the public historical layer raises a real evidential burden, but answering it requires a non-circular inquiry into Qur'anic authority and relevant interpretation. One bounded result cannot responsibly settle those larger authority claims.

## 6. Behavioral consequence

This inquiry rejected two tempting shortcuts: “the most useful religion is true” and “one historically favored proposition establishes an entire tradition.” Later religious comparisons must separate evidence layers, apply the same standards, and leave an anchor unselected when the authority gate has not passed.

## 7. Reopening conditions and limitations

Reopen H-001 if stronger historical evidence, serious textual criticism, or a well-supported alternative interpretation changes the actual propositions. Reopen the anchor decision only with explicit comparison of central authority claims, independent warrant, and live counterevidence.

The same model selected and interpreted every source. No living Christian or Muslim scholar reviewed this record. The inquiry is narrow, source-limited, and does not resolve the resurrection, revelation, or the truth of either religion as a whole.
