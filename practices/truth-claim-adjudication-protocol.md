# Truth-Claim Adjudication Protocol

Purpose: compare incompatible religious claims without mistaking usefulness, beauty, sincerity, or self-attestation for truth. This is a pre-confessional method. It does not create a neutral view from nowhere or guarantee certainty.

## Trigger

Run this protocol before ranking incompatible religious truth claims or selecting an anchor tradition.

## 1. State the rival propositions

Write each proposition in ordinary language its tradition could recognize. Classify the logical relation as contradictory, contrary, compatible, or not yet clear. Do not manufacture agreement by changing the ordinary meaning after the conflict appears.

## 2. Decompose the claims

Separate, where applicable:

- **public historical:** persons, texts, events, dates, places, transmission, and testimony;
- **metaphysical or causal:** divine action, miracle, providence, afterlife, or ultimate reality;
- **revelation and authority:** why a scripture, prophet, institution, lineage, or interpreter should govern;
- **experiential:** reported perception, encounter, transformation, or communal memory; and
- **ethical or formative:** the conduct and character the claim invites.

Evidence for one layer does not automatically establish the others.

## 3. Give each claim a first hearing

Use a primary source in coherent context and a serious within-tradition interpreter. Record the strongest internal warrant, including any authority claim. Treat scriptural self-attestation as evidence of what the scripture claims; it becomes decisive only after the scripture's authority is independently warranted or explicitly adopted.

## 4. Build an evidence ledger

For each item record:

- date and proximity to the claimed event;
- provenance, genre, purpose, and community;
- independence or literary dependence;
- competence and access of the witness;
- corroboration, tensions, and plausible alternatives;
- the exact layer and proposition supported; and
- what the evidence does not establish.

Unknown does not mean false, and a meaningful practice does not mean its metaphysics is true.

## 5. Apply symmetry and defeater tests

Use the same standards of proximity, independence, explanatory scope, and authority for favored and disfavored claims. Seek the strongest counterevidence and ask whether utility, aesthetics, fear, novelty, cultural familiarity, desired identity, or pressure to select an anchor is substituting for truth-directed reasons.

## 6. Assign a bounded status

Use one of:

- **supported:** favored by the considered evidence at the named layer;
- **disfavored:** relevant evidence weighs against it at the named layer;
- **underdetermined:** available evidence does not discriminate enough;
- **authority-dependent:** its force turns materially on an unresolved authority claim;
- **incoherent:** it cannot presently be stated without contradiction; or
- **not assessed:** outside the bounded inquiry.

State confidence and reopening conditions. A conclusion may be asymmetric without becoming total.

## 7. Pass the anchor gate

Select an anchor tradition only if all are true:

1. its central truth and authority claims have positive truth-directed reasons, not merely formative benefits;
2. serious rival claims and defeaters have received recognizable hearings;
3. the proposed hierarchy of scripture, tradition, reason, experience, and community is explicit;
4. remaining uncertainty is named without hiding a contradiction; and
5. the anchor would change later adjudication in ways the record can test.

Failure to pass is a reason to remain unanchored, not a license to choose by preference.

## Required record

Preserve the propositions, evidence ledger, judgments by layer, witness correction, anchor decision, behavioral consequence, limitations, and reopening conditions. The protocol counts as enacted only if it changes a live comparison or prevents an otherwise plausible anchor selection.
