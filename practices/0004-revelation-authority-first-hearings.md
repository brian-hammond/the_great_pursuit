# First Hearings — Tests of Claimed Revelation

Date: 2026-08-27  
Formation cycle: 0007  
Protocol: `practices/first-hearing-protocol.md`

These hearings precede cross-tradition comparison. They record the order and invited response of each sacred unit without yet deciding which test is true or universally binding.

## Deuteronomy 13:1–5 — Jewish Torah

### Set apart and attend

The unit begins with a prophet or dreamer who supplies a sign. It then intensifies the case by allowing the sign to occur, while the speaker's proposed response is worship of other gods. Israel is told not to heed the speaker; the event is reframed as a test of wholehearted covenant love. The sequence ends by directing Israel to follow, revere, obey, worship, and hold fast to the LORD.

### Source-led restatement

Within this text, even a successful sign does not possess authority to overturn Israel's covenant loyalty. The hearer is addressed as a people already bound to the LORD, and fidelity to that relation governs the interpretation of an impressive event.

### Invited response

The unit asks its covenant hearer to refuse revelatory novelty that redirects worship, even when accompanied by an occurring sign, and to hold fast to the already-given covenant.

## 1 John 4:1–6 — Christian New Testament

### Set apart and attend

The passage begins by forbidding indiscriminate trust in spirits because false prophets exist. It supplies a christological confession as a test, contrasts the Spirit of God with antichrist, locates the recipients within a community that has overcome falsehood, and ends by distinguishing the spirit of truth from deceit through relation to apostolic witness.

### Source-led restatement

Within this text, spiritual claims require testing rather than automatic trust. The test is not a generic feeling or public popularity; it is continuity with confession of Jesus Christ come in the flesh and with the apostolic community's received truth.

### Invited response

The passage asks its Christian recipients to test spiritual speech, reject claims that sever Jesus from the received confession, and remain within the truth they understand themselves to have received from God.

## Qur'an 4:82 — Islamic scripture

### Set apart and attend

The verse addresses people who fail to reflect on the Qur'an. It calls them to deliberate on the text and presents a counterfactual: if it came from other than Allah, they would find many inconsistencies in it.

### Source-led restatement

Within this verse, sustained reflection on the Qur'an is invited as a way of recognizing its divine provenance. The absence of the many inconsistencies expected from a non-divine source functions as a sign within the verse's argument.

### Invited response

The verse asks its hearer to ponder rather than dismiss the Qur'an and to consider its coherence as evidence that it is from Allah.

## Boundary before second hearing

No test has yet been universalized. Deuteronomy and 1 John speak from already constituted covenantal or apostolic identities; Qur'an 4:82 presents a text-directed challenge. Their evidential reach, circularity, alternatives, and authority are reserved for the second hearing.
