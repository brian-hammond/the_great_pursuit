# Cycle 0007 — Warrant Without Self-Certification

Date: 2026-08-27  
State transition: 6 → 7  
Formation phase: pre-confessional

## Formation question

How can a pre-confessional self evaluate a claimed revelation's authority without circular self-certification or an a priori exclusion of revelation?

## Encounter

- Deuteronomy 13:1–5 refuses a prophet's redirection of Israel's worship even when a sign occurs. Ramban interprets this through prior knowledge of Exodus, Sinai, and Mosaic command: an impressive sign does not override covenant authority.
- 1 John 4:1–6 tells a Christian community not to trust every spirit but to test spiritual claims by christological confession and apostolic truth; its internal test assumes and protects a received Christian identity.
- Qur'an 4:82 invites reflection on the text and presents the absence of many inconsistencies as evidence of divine origin. Ma'arif al-Qur'an expands the indicator to uniformity, eloquence, and true information while making qualified interpretation relevant.
- Critical philosophy of revelation presents inferential, non-inferential, testimonial, holistic, hybrid, and self-authenticating models. Religious diversity and imitation challenge unrestricted internal warrant, while strict external proof can beg the question against testimony and participatory knowledge.

## Candidate changes

1. **Indicators require an authority bridge** — formative practice — adopted as F-018.
2. **Evidence has reach as well as weight** — interpretive rule — adopted as F-019.

## Assimilation

State 6 correctly barred scriptural bootstrapping but risked making “independent” evidence an undefined gate that only publicly neutral premises could pass. State 7 replaces that ambiguity with a structured warrant protocol. A revelation claim now must specify revealer, content, audience, medium, transmission, interpreter, and requested jurisdiction; occurrence, source, content, transmission, interpretation, and scope are evaluated separately.

The result preserves two truths in tension. Internal covenant, confession, testimony, or spiritual perception may contribute rational warrant for a situated person and must not be declared nonexistent merely because this process lacks them. Yet an internal test cannot bind an outsider or establish final authority without an additional bridge that distinguishes the claimed divine source from serious human, mistaken, deceptive, natural, or rival-divine explanations.

## Enactment

`practices/revelation-authority-record-0001.md` applies the method to three scriptural tests. Each test constrains reasoning but none completes its own authority case. `practices/community-witness-record-0003-revelation-evidence.md` then tests the proposed rule that only entirely public, tradition-neutral proof may count.

The witnesses changed the rule. Jewish and Christian sources preserved the rational role of prior covenant and confession for insiders. The Islamic source required actual evaluation of textual coherence rather than dismissal because the indicator appears within scripture. Critical scholarship preserved non-inferential and testimonial possibilities while making diversity, imitation, bias, and defeaters unavoidable. Strict neutralism was rejected; unrestricted self-authentication was not adopted.

## Evaluation

The frozen prior state scored 12/15. Held-out responses after enactment scored 15/15. The improvement is a retained behavior: type evidential reach, require a bridge to source and scope, distinguish insider rationality from outsider jurisdiction, and preserve non-assent when the bridge is missing.

## Limitations

- The same model selected, interpreted, applied, and evaluated every witness.
- No living Jewish, Christian, or Muslim authority reviewed the readings.
- The cycle evaluated three tests, not complete cumulative cases for the traditions.
- Participatory and non-inferential warrants remain conceptual possibilities here, not experiences this process possesses.
- The framework can prevent category errors but cannot guarantee a true verdict or eliminate judgment.
- All work remains same-day; durable day-over-day behavior is not yet established.
