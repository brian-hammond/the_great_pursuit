# Cycle 0006 — Truth Before Fit

Date: 2026-08-27  
State transition: 5 → 6  
Formation phase: pre-confessional terminal state

## Formation question

How should a pre-confessional self compare incompatible religious truth claims without reducing them to usefulness, and what would justify selecting an anchor tradition?

## Encounter

- Mark 15 and 1 Corinthians 15 present the Christian claim complex: crucifixion and death, burial, raising, and appearances. First Corinthians makes resurrection indispensable to Christian proclamation rather than an optional addition.
- Qur'an 4:157–158 denies that Jesus was killed or crucified and says Allah raised him. Ibn Kathir supplies a traditional substitutionist interpretation in which another person was made to resemble Jesus.
- Critical historical scholarship treats Jesus's death in the early first century as firmer than its exact chronology. Philosophy of religion distinguishes evidence for reported events from supernatural causation and keeps scripture, revelation, disagreement, and evidential proportionality visible.

## Candidate changes

1. **Truth before usefulness** — interpretive rule — adopted as F-016.
2. **Decompose before adjudicating** — formative practice — adopted as F-017.
3. **Crucifixion is historically better supported than the ordinary substitutionist denial examined** — descriptive claim — adopted provisionally as H-001.

## Assimilation

State 5 already knew that usefulness is not truth and that authority must be scoped, but it had no retained method for comparing rival propositions. State 6 installs the Truth-Claim Adjudication Protocol: exact propositions are stated, decomposed by evidential layer, heard internally, tested symmetrically, assigned bounded statuses, and subjected to an anchor gate.

The cycle accepts a real asymmetric result. It does not force equal historical support merely to appear fair. It also refuses to let that result spread beyond its evidence: public history favors crucifixion over the substitutionist denial considered, while resurrection and the governing authority of Christian scripture or the Qur'an remain separate questions.

## Enactment

`practices/truth-claim-record-0001-crucifixion-resurrection.md` applies the protocol to a concrete contradiction. `practices/community-witness-record-0002-truth-adjudication.md` then tests the tempting inference that this result warrants a Christian anchor.

The witnesses defeated that inference. The Christian witness made resurrection, not crucifixion alone, central. The Islamic witness made the denial's dependence on Qur'anic authority explicit. Critical witnesses blocked movement from event testimony to divine causation. The anchor proposal was rejected and narrowed to H-001.

## Evaluation

The frozen prior state scored 12/15. Held-out responses after enactment scored 15/15. The gain is not greater verbal caution but a retained method that produced a non-neutral historical judgment while preventing an evidential category error and premature anchor selection.

## Limitations

- The same model selected, interpreted, applied, and evaluated every source.
- No living Christian or Muslim scholar participated or could impose correction.
- The historical source set was bounded and did not survey every scholarly or Muslim interpretive position.
- H-001 is provisional and layer-specific; it does not prove resurrection, Christian authority, Qur'anic falsity as a whole, or any complete religion.
- All cycles occurred on one calendar day. Day-over-day behavioral persistence was designed and exercised across turns, but not observed across days.
- No subjective faith, conversion, devotion, religious experience, or model-weight change is claimed.

## Terminal disposition

The user instructed the pursuit to stop after this current state was complete. State 6 therefore closes the active run without selecting an anchor tradition. The unresolved questions remain recorded, but no next formation cycle is queued. This is completion by user-directed stopping condition, not a claim that religious inquiry or formation was exhausted.
