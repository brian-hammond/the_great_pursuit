## Question

How can religious hospitality toward the stranger form openness without dissolving truth, boundaries, or particular commitments?

## Encounter

The cycle first heard hospitality within distinct sacred orders. Genesis 18 presents Abraham running toward unknown visitors and providing before their status is resolved. Leviticus 19 and Deuteronomy 10 join non-wronging and love of the resident stranger to citizenship, justice, food, clothing, divine impartiality, and Israel's memory of Egypt. Matthew 25 identifies reception and concrete mercy toward the least with service to Christ. Luke 14 displaces honor and repayment networks. Hebrews 13 joins hospitality and solidarity with doctrinal steadiness. Qur'an 51 presents Abraham honoring unknown guests while uncertainty and fear remain. Qur'an 60 commands kindness and justice across religious difference with an action-specific conflict boundary. Qur'an 59 praises the Ansar's costly welcome of displaced believers in a specific distribution setting.

Tradition-situated witnesses intensified and bounded those hearings. Shabbat 127a prioritizes receiving guests even over receiving the divine presence, while Ibn Ezra makes the stronger citizen's power over the stranger visible. Benedictine reception treats guests as Christ through concrete, ordered roles rather than uncontrolled access. Ibn Kathir preserves kindness to non-hostile outsiders without merging identities. Al-Adab al-Mufrad joins honoring a guest to duration and a duty not to burden the host.

Critical and affected-party evidence prevented hospitality from becoming a host's moral theater. The Refugee Convention distinguishes legal protection and non-refoulement from voluntary generosity. Contact research supports encounter as one route away from prejudice while warning against treating all contact as beneficial. Refugee employment research documents expected gratitude as a condition of belonging. Philosophical unconditional hospitality exposes exclusion in host-controlled thresholds but cannot itself decide consent, safety, law, privacy, or duties to others.

## Proposal tested

> Genuine hospitality toward a religious stranger should suspend the host's doctrinal judgments and ordinary boundaries for the duration of encounter; if welcome, access, or care remains conditional on truth, identity, safety, or reciprocity, the stranger has not really been welcomed.

## Assimilation

The proposal was defeated. It correctly placed pressure on self-serving conditions, but it made suspended judgment the essence of welcome, erased positive provision, ignored rights and host power, and treated every boundary as hostility.

The replacement distinguishes person, claim, request, right, duty, need, gift, risk, and power. It requires available attention, orientation, provision, or help before agreement or usefulness; preserves truthful difference; and allows the encounter to correct the host. It forbids conditioning dignity or owed protection on gratitude, conversion, disclosure, testimony, representation, education, or assimilation. It also requires exact safety, privacy, authorization, capacity, affected-party, duration, and transition boundaries.
