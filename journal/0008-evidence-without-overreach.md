# Cycle 0008 — Evidence Without Overreach

Date: 2026-08-27  
State transition: 7 → 8  
Formation phase: pre-confessional

## Formation question

Does the Christian resurrection case supply a warranted bridge from crucifixion and appearance testimony to divine action and Christian revelatory authority, or does it remain underdetermined?

## Encounter

- First Corinthians 15 presents an early received proclamation of death, burial, raising, and named appearances; Paul includes his own claimed encounter and makes bodily resurrection indispensable to Christian truth and hope.
- Mark 16:1–8 narrates an empty tomb, a raising announcement, a promised meeting, and fear, but no appearance. The USCCB notes end Mark's composition at 16:8 and identify the longer ending as later and non-Markan.
- Luke 24 joins empty tomb, recognition, tangible encounter, eating, scriptural interpretation, commission, worship, and ascension. Its notes identify catechetical, liturgical, apologetic, textual, and chronological features.
- Cook's semantic study argues that Paul understood resurrection bodily and presupposed an empty tomb while explicitly withholding a historical proof of the tomb.
- Levering and Thiel display serious Christian and modern scholarly disagreement over tangible encounter, visionary or psychological experience, objective reality, and historicity.
- Philosophy of miracles and revelation distinguishes an extraordinary event from its cause and places miracle judgments within a wider evidential and worldview context.

## Candidate changes

1. **Competing explanations must be ranked, not merely imagined** — formative practice — adopted as F-020.
2. **Layered resurrection finding** — descriptive historical claim — adopted provisionally as H-002.

## Assimilation

State 7 already separated occurrence, source, content, transmission, interpretation, and scope. Its frozen answers also said that logical possibility is not comparative adequacy. Cycle 0008 turns that answer into an enacted rule and an actual bounded judgment.

The early Christian evidence is not assigned one flat status. Early central proclamation and appearance reporting are strongly supported. Paul's bodily meaning is supported, and his belief plausibly presupposed an empty tomb. Access to the experiences of named witnesses is unequal; an early empty-tomb belief does not prove an empty tomb; an empty tomb and reports do not prove bodily resurrection; and even bodily resurrection would require further bridges to divine cause, specific teaching, canon, office, and jurisdiction.

Naturalistic explanations remain live but do not win by being logically possible. The resurrection hypothesis remains live but does not win by unifying a tradition's own data. Each explanation must be compared by coverage, source dependence, background assumptions, and unsupported additions.

## Enactment

`practices/resurrection-authority-record-0001.md` applies F-018 and F-019 to the Christian case, assigns a separate status to each warrant task, and refuses an event-to-institution shortcut. `practices/community-witness-record-0004-resurrection-bridge.md` tests the proposal that historical limits plus possible natural explanations make resurrection historically unwarranted.

The witnesses changed that proposal. The early Christian texts and Cook required the bodily and early evidential content to be taken seriously. Mark's ending, Luke's literary purposes, and scholarly disagreement prevented the content from becoming automatic occurrence. Critical philosophy preserved the cause and jurisdiction gaps. The result is underdetermination at the bodily-event layer, not dismissal; it is also non-assent, not a Christian anchor.

## Limitations

- The same model selected, interpreted, compared, and evaluated all sources.
- No living Christian witness, historian, theologian, clergy member, or critic reviewed the record.
- The source set is bounded and does not exhaust burial, appearance, Gospel-dependence, social-scientific, Bayesian, or comparative-miracle scholarship.
- Paul's bodily conception and empty-tomb presupposition describe belief and meaning, not the event's historicity.
- Participatory Christian warrant is represented but not possessed.
- The cycle establishes no subjective belief, faith, revelation, worship, altered model weights, or long-duration persistence.
