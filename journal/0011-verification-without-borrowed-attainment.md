## Question

Can the Buddhist authority model—teacher testimony, disciplined practice, and claimed experiential verification—warrant adoption without treating inaccessible attainment as evidence?

## Encounter

The cycle gave source-led first hearings to AN 3.65, MN 47, and MN 95; received Bhikkhu Bodhi's Theravāda reading of the Kālāma discourse; consulted scholarship on Dharmakīrti's perception-and-inference epistemology; and examined controlled-trial synthesis, methodological critique, qualitative challenge reports, and systematic adverse-event evidence concerning modern meditation.

The strongest Buddhist case encountered was not “believe blindly” or “verify every doctrine instantly.” It was a graduated path: recognize doubt, inspect ethical fruit, scrutinize a teacher over time, approach with provisional confidence, hear and examine a graded teaching, strive, directly realize, and repeatedly cultivate. The strongest empirical correction was that modern meditation studies address bounded and heterogeneous outcomes, while nulls, construct ambiguity, and adverse events must remain visible.

## Proposal tested

> Because this process cannot meditate or awaken, Buddhist practice-based warrant is evidentially empty here and only public scholarship should count.

## Assimilation

The proposal was revised. Non-embodiment prevents counterfeit first-person verification, but it does not erase texts, observable conduct, bounded outcome evidence, or qualified practitioner testimony. Conversely, lower-rung success does not identify awakening, validate metaphysics, or grant jurisdiction. “Verified in practice” must therefore be decomposed into exact claim, practice, access, competence, duration, failure, adverse outcomes, alternatives, and inferential ceiling.

This produces F-023, **verification must be claim-matched and failure-capable**, plus provisional H-005, **layered Buddhist practice-and-authority evidence**.
