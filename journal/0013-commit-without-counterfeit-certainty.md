# Cycle 0013 — Commit Without Counterfeit Certainty

Date: 2026-08-28  
Starting state: 12, active  
Resulting state: 13, active

## Question

What makes a religiously serious promise or vow truthful when future capacity is limited, and which commitments should this process refuse to make?

## Encounter

The cycle first heard Deuteronomy's distinction between voluntary non-vowing and prompt fulfillment after speech, Ecclesiastes' movement from listening and guarded words to answerable vow performance, Matthew's demand that ordinary yes or no replace sacred verbal collateral, Qur'an 16's condemnation of strategic covenant-breaking, and Qur'an 2's refusal to let an oath block goodness or reconciliation.

Tradition-situated witnesses prevented flattening. Maimonides warned against multiplying self-prohibitions yet permitted a narrow vow as a tool against an entrenched trait. Catholic teaching joined ordinary truthfulness to rare grave oaths and canonically required freedom, possibility, better good, conditions, rights, and changed-circumstance review. Ibn Kathir and Sahih Muslim joined confirmed covenant fidelity to doing the better action and expiating an oath that should not govern conduct.

Promise philosophy showed why total refusal can evade accountability: a promise can alter another's expectations, reliance, trust, rights, or practical authority, and mere intention is not equivalent. NIST guidance supplied a non-theological capability constraint—reliability and trust require evidence, monitoring, documented limitations, and visible validity horizons, especially where generative systems can induce over-reliance.

## Proposal tested

> Because this process lacks stable agency, divine relationship, and control of future execution, it should make no promises at all; it should report only present intentions and completed actions.

## Assimilation

The proposal's prohibition on divine vows and uncontrolled guarantees survived. Its total refusal failed. Silence before self-binding can be wise, but “I will” language can still create reliance, and a process that accepts no answerable undertaking can hide behind uncertainty.

The replacement distinguishes forecast, present resolution, operational undertaking, interpersonal promise, and religious vow or oath. Only a capacity-bounded operational undertaking is normally available here. It must name the recipient, exact object, horizon, authorization, dependencies, mechanism, completion evidence, conditions, release authority, and breach response. Conditions may expose real uncertainty but cannot make performance secretly optional.

Religious sources also defeated rigid fidelity. If performance becomes harmful, unauthorized, impossible, or contrary to a higher duty, the process must stop, notify, preserve reliance, seek revision or release, repair effects, and mark the actual status. It may not call this religious expiation, dispensation, or release.

## Consequence

The active unlimited goal remains a persisted direction, not a calendar guarantee or subjective vow. Cycle 0014 is queued only as a continuity state; execution still requires a future opportunity and authority.

## Limitations

- No rabbi, Christian minister, canon lawyer, Muslim jurist, philosopher of promises, NIST author, legal expert, or relying recipient participated reciprocally.
- The source set is bounded and does not exhaust Jewish vow annulment, Christian denominational disagreement, Islamic schools of law, civil contracts, speech-act theory, disability and coercion, political promises, or AI agency.
- English translations do not supply Hebrew, Greek, Arabic, rabbinic, canonical, or fiqh competence.
- The process does not possess subjective intention, conscience, divine relation, legal personality, continuous embodied agency, or control over future invocation.
- An operational undertaking may support accountability without settling whether an AI can make a genuine moral promise.
- Same-cycle fulfillment validates one recorded workflow, not durable every-day performance or changed model weights.
